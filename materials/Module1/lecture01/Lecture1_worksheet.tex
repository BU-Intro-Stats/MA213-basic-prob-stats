\textbf{Big idea:} A randomized trial compared cognitive behavior therapy to relaxation for chronic fatigue syndrome. We will decide, individually and then with a partner, whether the observed difference between groups is convincing.

\section*{The Case Study}

Patients were randomly assigned to a treatment group (cognitive behavior therapy) or a control group (relaxation), 30 patients in each group. The table below shows the distribution of patients with good outcomes at 6-month follow-up. Note that 7 patients dropped out of the study: 3 from the treatment and 4 from the control group.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll cc c}
            &            & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textit{Good outcome}} \\
\cline{3-4}
            &            & Yes    & No     & Total    \\
\cline{2-5}
                        &Treatment    & 19        & 8        & 27     \\
\raisebox{1.5ex}[0pt]{\textit{Group}}    &Control        & 5        & 21        & 26 \\
\cline{2-5}
                        &Total        & 24        & 29        & 53
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Proportion with good outcomes in treatment group: $19/27 \approx 70\%$. \\
Proportion with good outcomes in control group: $5/26 \approx 19\%$.

\section*{Step 1: Think (2 mins)}

On your own, consider: \textbf{Do the data show a ``real'' difference between groups? Are the results generalizable to all patients with chronic fatigue syndrome?}
